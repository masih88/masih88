### Ohm's law & power

*U, I, R, P – enter any two values* — `ohm`


### Voltage drop

*ΔU in V and %, losses, DE/CH limit* — `voltage_drop`

```text
ΔU = k · L · I · (ρ(θ)/A · cos φ + X' · sin φ)
k = 2 for DC/1~ (out and return), k = √3 for 3~ (line-to-line voltage)
ΔU % = ΔU / U · 100    P_loss = n · I² · ρ · L / A
Typically X' ≈ 0.08 mΩ/m for multi-core cables. 70 °C conductor temperature (PVC) is conservative; IEC 60364-5-52 Annex G uses ρ = 0.0225 Ω·mm²/m (Cu).
Limits: DIN 18015-1, DIN VDE 0100-520 Annex G, NIN – check against the edition in force and local rules.
```

**Inputs:** System, Nominal voltage U [V], Load current I_B [A], Cable length L (one way) [m], Cross-section A [mm²], Conductor material, Power factor cos φ, Conductor temperature θ [°C], Reactance per length X' [mΩ/m], Limit, Custom limit ΔU max [%]

### Disconnection / max. length (TN)

*Z_s,max and L_max for MCB B/C/D* — `disconnection`

```text
Disconnection in TN systems (IEC 60364-4-41, 411.4.4): Z_s · I_a ≤ U_0
Conventional method: L_max = 0.8 · U_0 · S_ph / (ρ · (1 + m) · I_a), m = S_ph / S_PE
0.8: 80 % of U_0 at the origin of the circuit; ρ = 0.0225 (Cu) or 0.036 (Al) Ω·mm²/m.
I_a = instantaneous trip of MCB: B 5 · I_n, C 10 · I_n, D 20 · I_n. For fuses take I_a from the curve.
Does not replace measuring the loop impedance.
```

**Inputs:** Voltage to earth U_0 [V], Protective device, Rated current I_n [A], Disconnection current I_a [A], Phase cross-section S_ph [mm²], PE cross-section S_PE [mm²], Conductor material, Planned length [m]

**Results:** Disconnection current I_a [A], Max. loop impedance Z_s [Ω], Max. cable length L_max [m]

### Pump/fan on VFD: affinity laws

*V̇ ~ n, Δp ~ n², P ~ n³ – power and saving with speed control* — `fan_affinity`

### Power factor correction

*Q_C and capacitor from cos φ1 to cos φ2* — `pf_correction`


### Transformer: currents & I_k

*Rated currents, ratio, short-circuit current* — `transformer`

```text
I = S / (√3 · U) for 3~ or S / U for 1~    ratio = U1 / U2
I_k ≈ c · I_r2 · 100 % / u_k   (infinite network, c = 1.05);  steady short-circuit current I_kD = I_r · 100 % / u_k (no c)
Typical u_k (Haag vol. 05 transformers, 2007): distribution transformers 10 … 20 kV 4 … 6 %, 110 kV 10 … 14 %, 380 kV 11 … 20 %. Haag example: 400 kVA, u_k = 4 % → I_r = 577 A, I_kD = 14.43 kA. “Stiff” transformers (small u_k) give higher short-circuit currents.
```

**Inputs:** Type, Rated power S_r [kVA], Primary voltage U_1 [V], Secondary voltage U_2 [V], Short-circuit voltage u_k [%]

### Valve kv/kvs & authority

*kv value, kvs selection, Δp, authority – also from power/ΔT* — `valve_kv`

```text
V̇ = P · 3600 / (ρ · c · ΔT)   (data of the valve list per VDI 3814 Part 4.1, Tab. 8: power, flow/return, ΔT)
kv = V̇ · √(ρ/1000 / Δp[bar])    Δp_V100 = (V̇ / kvs)² · ρ/1000 · 100 kPa
Valve authority a = Δp_V100 / (Δp_V100 + Δp_MV)
Δp_MV = pressure drop of the variable-flow section. Recommended a ≥ 0.5 (min. 0.3).
Water: ρ = 1000 kg/m³, c = 4.187 kJ/(kg·K). Glycol mixtures: take ρ and c from the manufacturer's data sheet.
Smallest controllable flow: V̇_min/V̇_100 = 1/√(1 + a·(S_v² − 1)), S_v = rangeability kvs/kvr; heat output with heat exchanger characteristic a_HX: Q̇/Q̇_100 = 1/(1 + a_HX·(V̇_100/V̇ − 1)). Example a = 0.5, S_v = 50: ≈ 3 % (no HX), ≈ 4 % (a_HX = 0.7). Two parallel valves 1/3 + 2/3 in sequence reduce the minimum output to about one third (QM Holzheizwerke, FAQ 37, 2015).
```

**Inputs:** Sizing from, Flow rate V̇ [m³/h], Power P [kW], Temperature difference ΔT (flow − return) [K], Medium, Density ρ [kg/m³], Specific heat c [kJ/(kg·K)], Desired Δp across valve [kPa], Δp variable-flow section [kPa], Rangeability S_v (kvs/kvr), Heat exchanger characteristic a_HX

### h,x chart (Mollier) & air processes

*Dew point, humidity, heating/cooling/humidifying/mixing in the chart* — `hx_diagram`

```text
Air pressure from the standard atmosphere: p = 1013.25 · (1 − 0.0065 · H / 288.15)^5.255 hPa
p_s by Magnus (WMO, over water): p_s = 6.112 · e^(17.62·t / (243.12 + t)) hPa    p_v = φ · p_s
x = 0.622 · p_v / (p − p_v)    h = 1.006 · t + x · (2501 + 1.86 · t) kJ/kg dry air
Dew point τ from p_v (inverse Magnus); wet bulb t_wb: h + (x_s(t_wb) − x) · 4.19 · t_wb = h_s(t_wb)
Heating/cooling: x = const.; below the dew point ideally saturated (x₂ = x_s(t₂), condensate ṁ · Δx)
Steam humidifier: t ≈ const.; evaporative humidifier: h = const.; mixing: x and h mass weighted
Power Q = ṁ · Δh, ṁ = V̇ · ρ₁ / 3600. Chart: oblique h,x coordinates (0 °C isotherm horizontal).
Below 0 °C saturation over water is used. Real coils: consider bypass factor / surface temperature.
```

### Controller tuning (Ziegler/Nichols, CHR)

*Kp, Tn, Tv from process data Ks, Tu, Tg – difficulty S* — `controller_tuning`

```text
Difficulty S = Tu / Tg (or Tt / T): < 0.1 easy, ≈ 0.2 medium, > 0.3 hard to control.
Ziegler/Nichols (1942, max. 20 % overshoot; single-capacity process with dead time Tt, time constant T):
P: Kp = T/(Ks·Tt) · PI: 0.9·T/(Ks·Tt), Tn = 3.3·Tt · PD: 1.2·T/(Ks·Tt), Tv = 0.25·Tt · PID: 1.2·T/(Ks·Tt), Tn = 2·Tt, Tv = 0.42·Tt.
Chien/Hrones/Reswick (1952, multi-capacity process, V0 = Ks·Kp):
aperiodic – disturbance: P 0.3; PI 0.6 / Tn 4·Tu; PID 0.95 / Tn 2.4·Tu / Tv 0.42·Tu – set point: P 0.3; PI 0.35 / Tn 1.2·Tg; PID 0.6 / Tn Tg / Tv 0.5·Tu;
20 % overshoot – disturbance: P 0.7; PI 0.7 / Tn 2.3·Tu; PID 1.2 / Tn 2·Tu / Tv 0.42·Tu – set point: P 0.7; PI 0.6 / Tn Tg; PID 0.95 / Tn 1.35·Tg / Tv 0.47·Tu (V0 factors each · Tg/Tu).
Source: Siemens “Regeltechnik – Grundlagen” (08/2022), ch. 3.3 and 6.5, p. 33–34 and 82–83 (Fig. 6-15, 6-18). Starting values – fine-tune on site.
```

**Inputs:** Process gain Ks, Delay time Tu or dead time Tt [s], Balance time Tg or time constant T [s], Controller type, Tuning rule

**Results:** Difficulty S, Controllability, Proportional gain Kp, Proportional band Xp = 100 %/Kp [%], Integral time Tn [s], Derivative time Tv [s]

## Building services & energy

### BAC efficiency class (EN ISO 52120)

*Savings from better building automation – BAC factors per building type* — `bac_efficiency`

```text
Energy new = energy old · f_BAC,new / f_BAC,old;  saving = 1 − f_new / f_old
f_BAC,th: heating and cooling; f_BAC,el: lighting and auxiliary energy (no other appliances).
Class C = 1 (standard reference), D worse, B and A better.
Source: Siemens “Building automation – impact on energy efficiency, application of EN ISO 52120” (09/2022), ch. 4.4, p. 124–125. Check against the edition of EN ISO 52120-1 in force.
```

**Inputs:** Building type, BAC class today, BAC class planned, Thermal energy today [kWh/a], Electric energy today [kWh/a], Price heat/cooling [€ / CHF], Price electricity [€ / CHF]

**Results:** f_BAC,th today, f_BAC,th planned, Saving thermal [%], Thermal energy planned [kWh/a], f_BAC,el today, f_BAC,el planned, Saving electric [%], Electric energy planned [kWh/a], Cost saving per year [€]

### Heating curve: slope & parallel shift

*Flow temperature vs. outdoor temperature, night setback* — `heating_curve`

```text
Slope s = (T_F(−5 °C) − T_F(+15 °C)) / 20 K, usual setting range 0.25 … 4.0.
Room set-point correction/setback: ΔT_F = ΔT_R · (1 + s) – parallel shift of the curve.
Support points: straight line through the points at −5 °C and +15 °C (linear grid as on the controller).
Design: T_F = T_i + (T_m,d − T_i)·q^(1/n) + (T_F,d − T_R,d)·q/2, q = (T_i − T_a)/(T_i − T_a,d), radiator exponent n ≈ 1.3 (floor heating ≈ 1.1).
Source: Siemens “Regeln und Steuern von Heizungsanlagen” (03/2023), ch. 3.1, p. 18–20 (example 67/33 °C, s = 1.7, −5 K → −13.5 K).
```

**Inputs:** Curve from, Flow at −5 °C [°C], Flow at +15 °C [°C], Room temperature (design) [°C], Design outdoor temperature [°C], Flow at design [°C], Return at design [°C], Radiator exponent n, Room set-point correction / setback [K], Current outdoor temperature [°C]

**Results:** Slope s, Flow at −5 °C [°C], Flow at +15 °C [°C], Flow shift ΔT_F [K], Shifted: flow at −5 °C [°C], Shifted: flow at +15 °C [°C], Flow set point at T_a [°C], Flow set point shifted [°C]

### Heat pump: COP, Carnot & SPF

*COP from temperatures, minimum COP per EN 14511, seasonal performance factor* — `heat_pump`

```text
COP_Carnot = T_sink / (T_sink − T_source) (temperatures in K); real COP ≈ efficiency grade · COP_Carnot.
COP = Φ_c / P_el (instantaneous value for one operating point);  SPF = heat / electricity over a year (kWh/kWh).
Minimum COP for the Keymark label (EN 14511): air/water > 3.1 (A2/W35), brine/water > 4.3 (B0/W35), water/water > 5.1 (W10/W35). Typical SPF (Fraunhofer 2011, AZ 2): air/water 2.9, brine/water 3.9.
Source: Siemens “Kältetechnik” (08/2022), ch. 7.6, p. 112–115. The efficiency grade is an assumption – use vendor data.
```

**Inputs:** Heat source, Source temperature [°C], Flow temperature (sink) [°C], Efficiency grade (assumption), COP from data sheet (test point), Heat produced per year [kWh/a], Electricity per year [kWh/a]

**Results:** COP Carnot (ideal), COP estimated (efficiency grade), Minimum COP Keymark (test point), Seasonal performance factor SPF, Typical SPF (field)

### Air handling unit & duct

*Velocity class (EN 13053), SFP class, duct diameter* — `ahu_sizing`

```text
Face velocity v = V̇ / (3600 · W · H) in the free unit cross-section.
Velocity classes EN 13053: V1 ≤ 1.6 · V2 ≤ 1.8 · V3 ≤ 2.0 · V4 ≤ 2.2 · V5 ≤ 2.5 m/s (above: V6 …).
SFP = P_el / V̇ in W/(m³/s); classes EN 13779 / EN 16798-3: SFP 1 ≤ 500, 2 ≤ 750, 3 ≤ 1250, 4 ≤ 2000, 5 ≤ 3000, 6 ≤ 4500, 7 > 4500.
Round duct d = √(4 · V̇ / (3600 · π · v)); low-velocity systems < 10 m/s in the duct.
Sources: TROX “Planungshandbuch RLT-Geräte” (2018), p. 6; Siemens “Einführung in die HLK- und Gebäudetechnik” (08/2022), ch. 6. Check the editions in force.
```

**Inputs:** Air flow V̇ [m³/h], Unit inner cross-section: width [m], Unit inner cross-section: height [m], Fan electric power [kW], Duct velocity [m/s]

**Results:** Face velocity [m/s], Velocity class, Specific fan power SFP [W/(m³/s)], SFP class, Round duct diameter [mm], Duct cross-section [m²]

### Reverberation time (Sabine) & DIN 18041

*Actual and target reverberation time, absorber area needed* — `reverberation`

